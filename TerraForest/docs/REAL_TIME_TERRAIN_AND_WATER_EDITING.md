# TerraForest real-time terrain and water editing

**Design and qualification record · 25 November 2026 · Milestone 5 open**

A player should be able to break ground, place terrain, empty a bucket or detonate a shoreline barrel and immediately interact with the resulting world. An author should be able to cut across a region boundary and see the whole cut, walk on its new floor and find water wherever the cut opened a connected cavity. The same operation must work from first person, strategy view and the dedicated authority. Water needs depth, a continuous shoreline and consistent volume. Realistic transient flow is outside the requested scope.

**Current verdict.** The packaged bird’s-eye editor can edit real terrain and finite water. Nearby terrain dig and fill changed the sampled collider on draw one. The latest full wet cut took 217 ms. In the corresponding shoreline run a sea blast took 30 ms and a lake blast took 47 ms. The full two-frame and steady-60-Hz gates remain open.

## The world editor is the primary workload

An author should be able to draw a channel from above, excavate its banks, deposit material to close one end, add or remove a measured quantity of water, then zoom down and walk or swim through exactly what they edited. A small stroke must preserve a cave roof or a narrow bank. A large stroke must work across region boundaries. Held, turning and interrupted strokes matter as much as individual clicks. The sea and lake blasts remain regression cases for an abrupt edit made through gameplay.

### Implementation and acceptance order

1. **Target the same world from either view.** Project the editor cursor through the active camera. Find the nearest eligible terrain, solid object or water boundary under that ray. Use a spatial index of published region bounds to narrow long overview rays before the precise surface test. The current water picker enumerates a short ray's bounding box, so extending its distance directly would make diagonal rays expensive. Retain hand-tool reach and authoring permissions at submission. A camera can request a small editable area at the target while the player's collision remains resident elsewhere. Test off-centre targets, occluded water, sky, stale targets and zooming mid-stroke.
2. **Use one brush command path.** Capture the target revision, endpoints, shape, radius, material or water quantity, and operation ID. Preview its world-space extent, validate the same bounds on the authority and commit the changed samples once. Preserve turns in held strokes, event order and quantities across cancellation, retries and reload. A finite water command records its actual source or sink. Digging redistributes existing water only into connected space. Deposition displaces it. An authored addition and a bucket transfer have different resource rules even if their visible result is identical.
3. **Budget the edited footprint.** Use changed terrain triangles and connected cavity capacities for interactive work. Publish collision, detailed water, picking and terrain for the same revision. Measure commands from single voxels through 64 m and 128 m diameter, on dry and flooded ground, plus a held stroke crossing several regions. Repeat a fixed stroke on lakes of different sizes. Report free-memory requirements, cells and bytes touched, p99 CPU/GPU, missed refreshes and input-to-draw latency. Measure a cold distant selection separately from an edit with resident data. Show progress and exact committed bounds for a larger management operation. Keep its full completion time visible.
4. **Qualify through normal controls.** In the packaged game use the editor UI to dig and fill a dry channel, admit water through a sill, close it, and directly add and remove a known quantity. Repeat in first person and overview with the avatar away from the target. Test the centre, boundary, neighbouring dry cavity, resting body, water level and submerged-object contact at draw two and after settling. Exercise the full 64 m cut, sea and lake barrels, interrupted buckets and save/reload on the same candidate. Preserve the established two-draw and 33.33 ms deadline for the full resident footprint, the 2 cm bank tolerance, 1 cm object tolerance and 60 Hz frame requirements. Record a held stroke's worst sample as well as its average.
5. **Require a human playtest for visual acceptance.** Keep a candidate independently selectable whenever it could degrade the image, the editing controls, or the way a stroke feels. A human must test that candidate against the reference before it becomes the default. Use the same save input, camera path, settings and lighting. Supply two matched views, underwater contact and a moving zoom/stroke sequence. In a disposable world, the reviewer should aim, drag, reverse zoom and walk or swim the result, checking shore gaps, object contact, terrain materials, silhouettes, seams, popping and perceived lag. Record the candidate and reference build IDs, reviewer's verdict, and any rejected scene. Both the engineering checks and an explicit human pass are required to promote such a change. Until someone runs that comparison, visual acceptance is pending.

## Which parts of volumetric water are worth keeping?

A still lake only needs a visible surface where air meets water. The game also needs to answer whether a point under that surface is in water, in solid terrain, or in a sealed dry cavity. When a barrel cuts a bank, the terrain supplies the new shape. A connection to the sea supplies its level. A finite lake additionally needs to account for the water available to fill the crater. A bucket must transfer the same quantity between the lake and the inventory. These requirements justify three-dimensional occupancy and a shared revision for rendering, collision and interaction. They do not require simulating waves or rebuilding the interior of a lake when someone digs.

### Implementation decision

Keep the water shader, finite quantities, save compatibility, contact checks and shared publication ticket. Keep the existing solver available as a correctness reference. Prioritize the existing basin-capacity design over further special cases in per-cell mesh extraction. Reusing terrain as the solid boundary avoids describing that boundary a second time. Each resident region should retain which cavities connect and how much water they hold below a given elevation. Replacing the summary for the edited region updates a finite basin's level without rediscovering its unchanged interior. The visible surface, bucket rays and immersion queries must all use the same water revision. A breached sill or newly sealed pocket requires an explicit transfer or split, including conservation through save and reload. [Basin-capacity design](WORLD_SYSTEMS_DESIGN.md#finite-shoreline-edits-following-the-november-20-packaged-trials)

Compare a basin-backed local surface with the reference first on a brush excavation and fill beside an unchanged lake outlet. Reuse the same comparison at the ordinary lake blast and after an interrupted bucket transfer. Keep the terrain-only cut as the independent control for changed triangles and real collision. Repeat the same local action on two different connected lake sizes before claiming that cost is independent of lake size. Adoption requires the editor workloads and human playtest above, conserved quantities, draw and wall-clock deadlines, and stable frame times. The comparison above measures the existing implementation. It establishes no timing for the proposed replacement.

## Implementation contract for parallel resident edits

A player command owns one terrain revision and a bounded set of changed samples. Terrain rendering, physics and detailed water must consume the same field. The local publication barrier covers only dependencies needed to play the edited footprint. A distant simulation job, a full-lake equilibrium job, archive I/O or GPU readback cannot be a prerequisite for this barrier.

| Owner | Required change | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Resident terrain field | Partition the complete visible edit footprint into bounded, revision-checked native readers. Share each reader between its terrain and water consumers. ## Where the current game spends time

**Paid terrain construction.** Right click enters the world input handler and starts terrain placement. A 3.5 m physics ray gives the hit and normal. Native command 39 checks 125 lattice samples and selects an exposed cell. The game checks reach, player and vehicle clearance, objects, permissions, selected inventory slot and available material. It debits one unit and submits command 38. A resident target can be read immediately. A busy worker or unavailable page can still delay admission. When a queued result arrives, the game revalidates the click so a changed aim or slot cannot spend material on the wrong cell.

**Terrain mining.** Left click starts the pickaxe swing. At contact the game resolves the live target and applies durability. A breaking hit enters the stroke buffer, passes water preflight and submits a terrain cut. A swing that does not break the target should not excavate it. The reward must correspond to the material actually removed.

**Publication.** Both operations enter the ordered terrain worker. It commits density, prepares the resident surface and sends geometry to the stream. The main thread prepares render and collision resources. At a wet boundary the terrain and water owners coordinate installation. The first draw showing the complete change is the player-visible result. Save and shutdown capture components at their own boundaries. A field-change notification can arrive substantially earlier than the first playable draw.

### From command to playable world

1. **One authoritative operation.** Local gameplay, strategy tools, dedicated hosting and trusted extensions submit a versioned command with an operation ID, world identity, target, affected revisions and required resources. Validate before mutation. Account for terrain samples, material, block removals, lost support, finite water and inventory exactly once. A rejected, cancelled or duplicate request must leave recoverable state. Subscribers receive post-commit events.
2. **Keep the player's editable area resident.** Maintain authoritative targeting and geometry data across the full reach of an equipped tool, including a new aim direction. Track field revision and ownership independently of the preview marker. A stale or unloaded region must yield a bounded readiness result. Separate simulation interest, collision readiness and camera detail so strategy zoom does not evict the ground under a player.
3. **Pass actual writes to geometry consumers.** Both the general brush and optimized large-cut writer must emit changed density and material sample runs and exact block removals. Expand them to incident cells, faces, normal dependencies, first-page effects and shared edges. Resolve shared edges from the same revision. Retain canonical vertices, material inputs and owner mappings between edits. Measure faces regenerated, collision owners replaced and bytes copied.
4. **Publish a playable region.** Prepare new geometry privately. At the physics and render handoff, remove superseded collision inside the edited footprint, retain unaffected support, wake resting bodies that lost contact and expose the matching visible surface. An old collider that still covers a new hole is a failure even if a targeting ray hits the new floor. Keep the prior complete surface through preparation or cancellation. Validate overlapping edits against the latest revision.
5. **Give adjacent water the same boundary.** Use the committed terrain and structure revision for water occupancy, detailed surface, picking and immersion. A finite lake owns a conserved quantity. Cache regional capacity and outlet connectivity so a local edit can update the affected contribution. A sea exchanges volume with a fixed-level boundary. A sealed cavity below sea level stays dry until a connection opens. Closing an outlet, displacing water with construction and interrupting a bucket operation must preserve the correct accounting. Marching cubes can extract a surface from a field. The field, connectivity and volume accounting determine where water belongs.
6. **Separate subsequent work.** Publish local playability independently of far meshes, vegetation presentation and wider equilibrium. Save and replicate committed terrain, water and revisions. Rebuild derived resources from that state. Keep queues bounded, cancel superseded work by revision and share one active simulation between players interested in the same region. Handle unavailable dependencies and interrupted saves without replacing a player world.

## Why this design and what can disprove it

The measured full cut affected 36 owners. In an earlier paired trial, 45,367 of 56,610 final render triangle records changed. At that run's presentation phase, 11.21 ms for density and 12.15 ms from commit to draw left **9.97 ms** of the 33.33 ms allowance for the other serial work. Regenerating every changed record in that interval would require at least **4.55 million records per second**, before collision and water. These figures set a feasibility target. A centre-only preview cannot satisfy the footprint probes 24 m from the centre.

Reinstalling already-prepared resources for all 36 owners took 0.59 ms in a separate replay. That excludes extracting fresh geometry and preparing new physics resources. The replay also exposed a sleeping body that kept its old support for seven physics ticks. Splitting terrain into 8 m triangle pages increased the measured post-commit draw time from 127.27 to 159.94 ms in another paired run. Those results favour measuring the generation and retirement of an exact local replacement before adopting smaller pages globally.

**First choice:** retain ordinary engine colliders and render the exact local boundary from resident data, including the perimeter. Measure the full cost of replacing every collision owner crossed by that boundary. If this cannot meet the gate, evaluate direct density-field contacts and rendering against identical sweeps, rigid bodies, materials, shadows, seams and timings. Adopting a custom physics backend also requires a maintainable dedicated-server and export path.
