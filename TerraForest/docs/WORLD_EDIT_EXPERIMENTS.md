# World Editor experiments and architecture decisions

**25 November 2026 · Milestone 5 is open.** A player must see and be able to use the same edited ground and water across the entire resident brush footprint by the second draw and within 33.33 ms. First input feedback is due on the next draw. Record both draw count and elapsed time. The rendered client also has a separate 60 Hz frame-pacing requirement. A single 30 ms frame can meet the edit deadline and still miss a refresh. [Qualification contract](WORLD_SYSTEMS_DESIGN.md#real-time-terrain-and-water-editing)

## What excavation does today

The editor picks terrain under the pointer, queues a sphere, box or voxel in the shared stroke buffer, and submits the accepted terrain edit. In wet terrain the water system invalidates occupancy against the new field, prepares the local redistribution and publishes water with the terrain revision. The explicit Remove water tool takes a different path and debits the finite store. There is currently no combined terrain-and-water authoring toggle. [Editor input](../addons/player_runtime/world_editor.gd) · [Brush submission](../demo/controller.gd) · [Water settlement and explicit edits](../addons/volumetric_water/lake_world.gd)

In a packaged read-only Cedar Vale session, a normal World Editor click with a 2 m radius lowered the lake floor from 41.428 m to 38.656 m. The water surface fell from 44.1610 m to 44.1573 m. A terrain fill raised the floor to 41.093 m and the surface to 44.1601 m. All three measurements had exactly **27,698,261,603 mL** of owned water. The physical floor 4 m away stayed at 41.435 m while the waterline there moved too. At the centre, 1 m and 4 m, the rendered-surface query and the immersion height agreed after settlement. Both strokes still showed the old floor at draw two. This reproduces redistribution and delayed publication for this location. An apparent loss elsewhere remains a reproducible bug candidate, particularly near an outlet or after interrupting a stroke. [Packaged editor measurements](../reports/checks/20261125T170201324070Z/console.log)

The native bank test increased the length of a connected lake eightfold, from 16 to 128 regions. The first excavation in both fixtures visited 13 cells in two regions and left 56,529 mL in the edited bank. Deposition visited 12 cells in two regions and displaced the water from the filled cell. The disconnected pocket retained its original bytes. In contrast, the packaged 64 m wet cut sampled 456 occupancy regions, prepared 1,800 prospective receiving regions and generated 589 render packets in an instrumented run. A bounded response already works for the simple bank fixture. The cost of a large excavation requires a separate experiment. [76 native checks](../reports/checks/20261125T170247398548Z/console.log) · [Packaged work counts](../reports/checks/20261125T162043367901Z/console.log)

The repeat package `02d7a2ad0c4efebd` recorded the terrain revision on draw three at 45.647 ms for Dig and on draw four at 50.658 ms for Fill. Its checks explicitly failed the two-frame bound for both actions. The earlier run recorded 42.328 ms and 52.730 ms respectively. On the repeat package, a **dry** 64 m cut staged its terrain batch at 49.392 ms and reached the first post-commit draw at 84.649 ms. At draw two, 25.280 ms after input, zero of nine changed collider rays matched the finished floor. The dry and wet controls use different locations. The dry result establishes that terrain alone misses the deadline. Subtracting the two durations would not isolate the cost of water. ## Editor controls and water ownership

The proposed bottom toolbar has four directly selectable tools, **Dig**, **Fill**, **Add water** and **Remove water**. The active tool shows its applicable shape, radius, material or amount. Keep the world cursor visible, left-click or drag to edit, middle-drag to pan at a speed proportional to zoom, and use the wheel to zoom. The camera can cross the map independently of the character. Toolbar clicks and pan or zoom gestures must generate zero world edits. Future world tools use the same selection and capture rules. The current preview already supports the separate cursor, pan and zoom. Its tool selector is in the upper panel. [Current controls](PLAYER_UI.md#birds-eye-world-editor-preview)

**Also edit water** is an explicit, default-off option on Dig and Fill. Water always responds physically to a terrain change. The option grants an editor stroke permission to create or remove finite water. Ordinary mining, construction and explosions keep their existing resource rules.

| Stroke | Option off | Option on |
|---|---|---|
| Dig | Excavation opens capacity. Existing connected water can enter it. A sealed dry pocket stays dry. The authored water delta is zero. | Remove the water actually present inside the swept brush immediately before excavation. Record the exact debit once. Publish the new terrain and redistribute the remaining water. A connected lake or sea can subsequently refill the opening. |
| Fill | Solid excludes water. Displace existing water into connected capacity and account for any reservoir exchange. The authored water delta is zero. | Apply the terrain fill and account for displaced water. Create water only in the **remaining open space inside that same brush**, up to its available capacity. Subtract water already there. A fully solid stamp creates exactly zero. Dry space outside the brush receives no direct addition. |

Capture the option, shape, swept endpoints and operation ID **when the stroke is sampled**. Use the same geometric footprint, including partial cells, for terrain and water. Stage the signed water delta against the old and new occupancies and expose the terrain, collider, water queries and detailed surface under one committed revision. A failed or stale command applies neither half. Replaying an operation ID cannot change inventory or water twice. Held strokes must account for their swept union and must not repeatedly remove water that just flowed back into an overlapping stamp in the same stroke. A click with the toggle enabled can have a water effect even if its terrain samples are already at the requested shape. Close the active stroke on tool change, toggle change, loss of focus, or start of navigation. Treat the sea as an explicit source or sink and report its exchange independently of the authored delta. [Shared authority and persistence contract](WORLD_SYSTEMS_DESIGN.md)

For each finite operation, verify in integer millilitres that **change in owned world water + change in carried water = authored addition − authored removal + signed reservoir exchange**. Record displaced and redistributed water separately for diagnosis because they move existing quantity. Keep the option and its permission on the versioned authority command as well as the toolbar, with the default applying to existing clients and saves. Leave the combined control out of the playable preview until both Dig and Fill pass this test through normal controls.

## Mini-tests that decide the redesign

Start with the captured wet stroke and the dry 64 m cut. Advance from the smallest failing workload to the larger workload only after the candidate passes the smaller one. Each comparison uses the same edit, starting revision, save, render settings and camera. Count queue age, bytes copied or uploaded, peak live bytes, regions and faces touched, and the wall time of each dependency. Worker CPU totals may overlap. Use matched control and candidate runs. Capture images in a separate pass so encoding does not enter the frame-time distribution.

| Order | Smallest experiment | Observation that determines what to build |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Disappearing water | Repeat the editor Dig and Fill over a submerged floor, a shallow bank, an outlet and a sealed cavity. Record the physical floor, detailed surface, immersion and owned mL before input, at draw two and after settlement. Interrupt once with a bucket. Compare sea exchange separately. | A changed balance is an accounting defect. A correct balance with missing surface over occupied water is a mesh or publication defect. A lowered surface with consistent depth and quantity is redistribution. The submerged floor route is executable today. Add the other geometries as regressions before shipping the toggle. |
| 2. Combined brush arithmetic | Use one 0.5 m cell of 125,000 mL capacity. Start a Dig with 60,000 mL in it. Test Fill on a completely solid stamp and on a clipped pocket with 62,500 mL of post-edit capacity and 12,500 mL already retained. Add a sloped cell, a partial sphere, a box crossing a region edge and a held overlap. Repeat the same operation ID, cancel and reload. | Expected authored deltas with the option on are −60,000, 0 and +50,000 mL in the three single-cell cases. With the option off each authored delta is zero. For a diagonal sweep, account for the intersection of the brush and the wet or open subvolume inside a boundary cell. Multiplying a whole-cell capacity by the fraction of brush coverage can give the wrong answer on a slope. Verify displacement independently. Fail closed if exact capacity or authority is unavailable. Route every view through the same finite-water transaction before enabling the control. |
| 3. Dry terrain and real physics | Start with a 4 m dig and deposition, then an unpredicted dry 64 m cut. Replay prepared meshes and colliders once to measure installation alone. Compare this with generation of newly changed triangles and a playable replacement collider. Include boundary and exterior rays, the avatar capsule and moving and sleeping bodies. | If installation alone misses, change how physics shapes are owned and replaced. If fresh extraction dominates, keep stable triangle ownership and replace changed faces and contact data. If dense edits still exceed budget, trial direct collision against the resident density field with the same sweeps and resting bodies. Require equivalent support and retirement of old faces for every candidate. [Existing terrain alternatives and failed partition trials](WORLD_SYSTEMS_DESIGN.md#full-cut-decision-from-the-packaged-replay) |
| 4. Lake size and topology | Hold brush shape and position constant. Increase the connected lake length eightfold in the native fixture, then repeat in the game with two basin sizes. Test an unchanged outlet, a newly breached sill and a split or sealed pocket. Compare results with full settlement. | If work grows with the unchanged interior, implement persistent per-region cavity capacities by elevation and face connections. Invalidate edited cells and changed outlets. Use the smallest complete connected domain needed for a split. Measure cold summary construction, memory per region, peak temporary storage and recovery. The first cold edit must meet its own deadline. |
| 5. Worker completion to visible water | On the ordinary lake blast, timestamp accepted terrain, immutable field, worker completion, main-thread adoption, successful physics installation and first matching draw. Compare current scheduling with immediate handoff of a completed revision. Check a bucket interrupt, stale completion and repeated edits. | Earlier lake measurements put the worker at 25.74 ms and staged water at 33.84 ms, leaving no time for the matching draw. Removing that handoff gap is worth measuring on small edits. On the 64 m cut, terrain alone took 55.43 ms and water alone took 183.865 ms to stage. Scheduling alone cannot close either large-cut gap. [Lake timing](../reports/checks/20261125T163656891750Z/normal_gameplay_blast_performance.json) · [Cut timing](../reports/checks/20261125T163618831305Z/console.log) |
| 6. Rendering backend decision | Feed identical accepted field changes into the CPU boundary path and an opt-in GPU surface builder. Separately replay the same edit on a simulation-only authority. Time CPU extraction, GPU execution, upload, synchronization, collider availability and the first playable draw. Repeat with a second edit while the first is in flight. | Adopt GPU generation where end-to-end publication, memory and frame pacing improve at identical detail. Keep authoritative quantities and real collision available without a GPU. If independent CPU and GPU derivations disagree at a seam, keep the shared boundary until the disagreement is resolved. |
| 7. Editor and full-game gate | Exercise the bottom toolbar, click through its empty margins, pan through a region boundary, reverse zoom during a held stroke, and edit 211 m from a stationary avatar. Repeat the 64 m wet cut, both gameplay shoreline barrels, mining and paid deposition. | Check input-to-feedback, commit, actual triangle contact, collider, immersion and picking from two fixed angles, plus submerged object contact. Run three cold and three warm routes at 1080p. Keep p99 CPU and GPU work at or below 13.3 ms each, check actual presentation at 60 Hz and count every game-attributable miss. Require a human A/B playtest for any visual or interaction change before promotion. |

**Decision order.** Fix any demonstrated quantity or contact defect first. Run the dry-physics and water-capacity experiments independently. A scheduling change qualifies only for workloads it moves inside the measured deadline. Keep ordinary gameplay and the dedicated host on one authority contract through any rewrite. For the measured 216.865 ms wet cut, reaching 33.33 ms requires about a **6.5×** end-to-end improvement. Even with everything else free, the measured terrain and water staging times need at least **1.66×** and **5.52×** speedups respectively. The actual requirement is higher because a matching draw still has to occur. Stable 60 Hz, memory plateau and multiplayer capacity remain separate qualification results. ## Reproduction

From the TerraForest project directory, these entry points use the admitted headless or isolated render paths. The packaged editor driver loads Cedar Vale read-only, uses viewport input, and verifies the original save hash at exit. A failed timing assertion is retained alongside the successful quantity and contact checks.

```sh
python tools/run_godot_check.py water_equilibrium_scope --headless --timeout 240 --require " failures=0"
python tools/check_release_water_edits.py --route overview_wet --route dry_cut
python tools/check_release_water_edits.py --route cut --route shores --route bucket_interruption
python tools/check_release_water_edits.py --route cut_angles --route shore_angles --route object_contact
```
