WORK IN PROGRESS -- shared runtime surface-nets adoption

Water next_mesh now uses the same crossing aggregation, dual-cell connectivity
and triangulation as terrain. Water supplies exact interval boundary crossings
so a one-litre layer does not vanish between ordinary density samples. This is
the sole water runtime mesh builder; custom top/side/shore assembly is removed.
Authoritative volume storage, flow, queries and archives were not replaced.

Final world run: 49 checks passed, including mining, buckets, conservation and
cached save/reopen. Native mesh check: 30 thin-layer/boundary fixtures passed.
Terrain regression: 72 complete packets match the frozen pre-refactor version.

NOT COMPLETE: moving publication cracks remain; queries still use old shoreline
polygons and differ from the new reconstruction. Older continuity tests failed
seven assertions before removal of the vertical-publication gate. Some tests
assume the previous vertex layout or queue order, but diagonal/ledge isolation
and invalid-region behavior still require meaningful replacement coverage.
Do not discard those requirements merely because new tests passed.

Warm loading revealed a dependency cycle: meshes awaited vertical repairs while
loading paused simulation awaiting meshes. Shared reconstruction no longer needs
that old mesher gate. Invalid terrain occupancy still defers local publication.
Empty storage neighbours can own boundary faces and now receive render jobs,
including on restore. These jobs do not allocate water storage regions.

No sustained 60 FPS, thermal sustainability or scale claim. Water storage and
world streaming are not unified by this geometry change. The recorded native
continuity failures are retained intentionally, not presented as a pass.
