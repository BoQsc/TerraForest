# Name brief — Dead Essence

Status: decision, user-selected. The game folder still uses `TerraForest`; no brand files were changed.

## Brief

Grim, serious, cinematic zombie survival. Biological experiments. Procedural worlds with their own histories. Large persistent multiplayer. Progression from one struggling survivor to a fortified settlement of survivors. Atmospheric apocalypse, no comedy, no cartoon tone.

## Decision

**Dead Essence**

It is the stronger title for this game than the alternatives because it works by mood rather than explanation.

- **It sounds like a horror title, not a design document.** Two words, three syllables, a hard stop, and an ominous phrase that survives being said aloud in a lobby.
- **"Essence" is biological and metaphysical at once.** It can be a substance, a residue, a memory, a pathogen, or the thing the dead leave behind. The ambiguity is the hook, not a flaw.
- **"Dead" sets the genre without explaining the premise.** Players know the register immediately; the second word is what makes it specific.
- **It scales.** It works for a lone survivor, a settlement, a generated world, and a persistent server without sounding like a feature list.
- **It leaves the world mysterious.** The title does not tell you whether the dead are infected, reclaimed, or something else. That is useful for a game about discovering a generated world's own history.

## What the previous recommendation got wrong

The earlier pick optimized for storefront distinctiveness and literal genre signaling. That produced names that were clever on a slide and cold in the mouth:

- **Blightwake** sounded like an invented fantasy compound and explained the plague rather than evoking it.
- **Cultivar** had a smart double meaning but read as farming or colony sim before the horror landed.
- **Witherhold** sounded like a mobile strategy game.
- **Hollow Season** was atmospheric but soft, and the name is already occupied.
- **Carrion Bloom / Rotbloom** were visceral but try-hard.

Originality is not the only test. A familiar phrase with the right mood can beat a unique phrase with no pulse.

## Collision reality

- No exact Steam app was found for **Dead Essence**.
- Prior uses exist but are small: a 2015 Unity survival-game studio project, a 2001 music release, and a Roblox badge.
- The phrase is not saturated like *Dead Space*, *Dead Island* or *Dead Frontier*.
- These are storefront checks, not trademark clearance. A real launch still needs trademark, domain and social-handle checks.

## Make the title stronger

Define the phrase in-world so it stops being abstract:

- **Dead Essence** is the substance or residual biological agent recovered from the dead, or the name of the experiment that produced it.
- Documents, audio logs and server events can refer to *the Essence* the way a conspiracy refers to a material.
- The player's first encounter with the phrase should explain nothing and imply everything.

Presentation:

- Wordmark: **DEAD ESSENCE** in a cold serif or clinical sans, with wide tracking and no tagline crowding it.
- Palette: bone white, black, rust, sickly green; microscopic and post-mortem imagery rather than cartoon gore.
- Avoid a subtitle unless marketing demands one; the two-word title is already complete.

Taglines that fit:

- What the dead leave behind.
- Nothing dies clean.
- The end is not empty.
